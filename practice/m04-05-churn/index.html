Reading any output the same way

A number with no unit, no baseline and no direction tells a head of retention nothing. The product of your analysis is not 0.3639. It is one sentence they can act on: "We catch 143 of the 393 customers who leave, and we flag 207 customers to do it." Reading every output in the same order is how you stop yourself from misreading it, and how you catch a broken network before the retention team does. The Look for box in A5 and A8 to A10 uses these five labels.

LabelAsk yourself
UnitsWhat is the number measured in? A share of a group (recall 0.3639 is 36.4 of every 100 churners), a count of customers (207 flagged) or dollars. Say which group: of the 393 who left, of the 2,000 in the test rows.
BaselineCompared with what? Saying "Stayed" for everyone is 80.35% accurate and catches nobody. A coin flip has an AUC of 0.50. For a neural network, the bar is the logistic regression.
DirectionWhich way does it lean? A lower threshold flags more customers: recall goes up and precision goes down. Which mistake is the costly one?
Size in dollarsTurn counts into dollars with a price you can name. Is the gap big enough to change the decision?
SentenceWho is it for, how many customers, how many dollars, and what should they do?

The numbers in one line each

  • Accuracy: the share of all customers the model got right. It hides the leavers, because most customers stay.
  • Precision (Churned): of the customers the model flags, the share that really leave. How trustworthy the call list is.
  • Recall (Churned): of the customers who really leave, the share the model flags. How many leavers we catch.
  • Threshold: the cut-off on the churn probability (0.5 unless you change it). Lower means more customers flagged.
  • AUC: how well the model ranks customers from safest to riskiest, from 0.5 (a coin flip) to 1.

# Helper functions
def cramers_v(x, y):
    """Cramér's V: association between two categorical variables (0–1)."""
    ct = pd.crosstab(x, y)
    chi2 = chi2_contingency(ct)[0]
    n = ct.sum().sum()
    r, k = ct.shape
    return np.sqrt(chi2 / (n * (min(r, k) - 1)))

def cohens_d(group1, group2):
    """Cohen's d: effect size between two groups."""
    n1, n2 = len(group1), len(group2)
    pooled = np.sqrt(((n1-1)*group1.std()**2 + (n2-1)*group2.std()**2) / (n1+n2-2))
    return (group1.mean() - group2.mean()) / pooled if pooled > 0 else 0
